This is a rules-assisted diagnostic for mus_8r8a_federation_no_senas_v2,
not a strategic game turn. Hypothetical cards explicitly supplied in a question
are available for that question only. Physical ranks are 1,2,3,4,5,6,7,10,11,12;
four suits each. Physical 2 compares as ace (1), and 3 compares as king (12).
Juego values are 1 for ace/2, 10 for 3/10/11/12, and face value for other cards.

North/South are team 0; East/West are team 1. Table priority is North, East,
South, West, wrapping around from mano. The first eligible seat in that order
starts each lance. Equal hands favor the first eligible seat in mano order.
Each team's best eligible individual hand competes; partners' cards are not merged.

Grande compares normalized ranks sorted descending, at the first difference.
Chica compares ranks sorted ascending; lower is better at the first difference.
Pares: duples (two pairs or four equal) beat medias (triple), which beat simple
(one pair). Compare only pair/triple ranks. Duples compare high pair then low pair;
four equal count as two pairs of that rank. Singletons do not break pares ties.
Juego qualifies at total 31 or above, ordered 31,32,40,37,36,35,34,33.
Equal juego totals tie. Punto is played only if nobody has juego; higher total wins.
Pares/juego declarations are truthful and only eligible seats bet. If just one
team qualifies, it wins that lance without betting. No pares means skip pares.

Intrinsic points: pares simple=1, medias=2, duples=3 per eligible hand; juego
31=3 and other qualifying totals=2. Sum the winning team's eligible hands.
Passed grande/chica/punto pay 1. Called grande/chica pay only the numeric stake;
called pares/juego add intrinsic values; called punto adds 1.

A pass declines to open bidding but does not remove eligibility. Bets and raises
name TOTAL stakes. Opening minimum is 2; raises increase the total by at least 2.
The next eligible opposing seat gives one binding team response. Numeric accepted
bets wait until ordinary showdown; a numeric bet equal to the target is not ordago.
Folding an opening bet pays the bidding team 1 immediately. Folding a raise pays
the previous numeric stake immediately, with no extra point. A fold also concedes
that team's pares/juego intrinsic values or the punto point at showdown, if play
continues. Already-paid points are not paid again. Ordinary showdown reveals all
four hands, then settles grande, chica, pares, juego/punto, stopping when a team
reaches the target. The displayed score contains only points already paid.
An ordago offers an immediate showdown for the match and permits only call/fold.
Calling it makes the best eligible hand's team win immediately, cancelling pending
awards. Its winner's score is raised to the target, not awarded target extra points.
Folding ordago follows the usual refusal rule.

Mus votes are sequential and unanimous. Any no_mus starts grande immediately.
After unanimous mus, each seat exchanges 1–4 cards and then voting restarts.
Discard indices refer to the current hand; kept cards precede replacements.
Discards may return through recycling. There is no game-rule limit on exchanges.

A policy observation includes its own current cards, its own discarded cards
within the current hand, public cumulative discard counts, public events,
paid scores, pending public settlement terms, and the legal-action mask.
It excludes the seed, stock order, other seats' private discards, and other seats'
unrevealed hands. Full public history, including prior showdown cards, persists.
Own private discard recall resets at a new hand; this is not perfect lifetime recall.
Pending terms do not contain a hidden-hand comparison result, although a fold's
winning team or an uncontested team may already be known publicly.

An environment episode terminates on a natural match end, or a completed hand
when hand episodes are configured. An external step budget instead truncates an
unfinished episode, without inventing a winner or completed-hand return. Natural
termination takes precedence if it coincides with the step limit. Reset is required
before stepping again after either outcome.
